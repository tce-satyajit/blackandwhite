Grade 8 – 10 (Middle & Upper Secondary Science / Physics / Design & Technology)

Subject: Science / Physics / Design & Technology

Relevant Units / Chapters:

Oscillations & Periodic Motion: Oscillation, Amplitude, Time Period and Frequency ($f = 1/T$), Reading a Period off a Real Oscillator, Why Any Clock Is an Oscillator Plus a Counter, Beats as Half an Oscillation.

Simple Harmonic Motion: The Restoring Force on a Pendulum Bob ($F = -mg\sin\theta$), The Small-Angle Approximation ($\sin\theta \approx \theta$), Why That Approximation Is What Makes the Motion Simple Harmonic, Force Proportional to Displacement and Directed Towards the Centre.

The Simple Pendulum: The Period Formula ($T = 2\pi\sqrt{L/g}$), What the Period Does Not Depend On (Mass, Amplitude, Material), Rearranging to Find the Length for a Chosen Period, The Seconds Pendulum and Why It Is 994 mm, Using a Pendulum to Measure $g$ ($g = 4\pi^2 L / T^2$).

Gears, Ratio & Turning Effect: The Gear Rule ($N_1 n_1 = N_2 n_2$), Teeth per Second as the Quantity That Must Match, Compound Ratios Multiplying Through a Train, Reading a Ratio Backwards from Hand to Escapement, Gearing Down Multiplies Turning Effect and Divides Speed by the Same Factor, Moment of a Force ($\tau = F \times r$).

Energy & Its Transfer: Gravitational Potential Energy ($E_p = mgh$) and Kinetic Energy ($E_k = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$) Trading Places Every Swing, Elastic Potential Energy Stored in a Wound Spring, Conservation of Energy versus Dissipation as Heat, Why an Undriven Pendulum Runs Down, What the Escapement Actually Does.

Friction & Resistive Forces: Air Resistance and Pivot Friction as the Reason a Clock Needs Winding at All, Why a Suspension Spring Is Used Instead of a Bearing, Amplitude as the Balance Point Between Energy In and Energy Lost.

Thermal Expansion: Linear Expansion of a Solid ($\Delta L = L\alpha\Delta\theta$), Coefficient of Linear Expansion for Brass, Turning a Length Change into a Timekeeping Error, Why Warm Clocks Run Slow, Compensated Pendulums as an Engineering Answer.

Measurement, Precision & Error: Systematic versus Random Error, Percentage Error, The Resolution of an Adjustment (One Turn of the Rating Nut), Calibrating an Instrument Against a Standard, Reading Small Differences by Letting Them Accumulate Over a Day, Why 43 Seconds a Day per Millimetre Is a Sensitivity.

Circular Motion & Rotational Kinematics (Cross-Curricular): Revolutions per Minute, Angular Displacement in Degrees and Radians, Six Degrees per Second on a Seconds Hand, Angular versus Linear Quantities.

Mathematics (Cross-Curricular): Ratio and Proportion, Direct and Inverse Proportion, Square-Root Relationships and Why Halving a Quantity Does Not Halve Its Root, Rearranging a Formula to Make a Different Quantity the Subject, Small-Change Approximations ($\Delta T/T = \frac{1}{2}\Delta L/L$), Working in Base 60.

Design & Technology: Anatomy of a Mechanical Movement — Frame, Arbors, Wheels, Pinions and Pivots, Why a Clock Wheel Is Crossed Out, The Fusee as a Mechanical Feedback Device, Escapement Types and What They Trade Off, Reading a Mechanism by Following the Drive from Spring to Hand.
