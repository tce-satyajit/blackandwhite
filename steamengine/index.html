Walschaerts Valve Gear — How Steam Drives the Wheels

Timing the steam so a double-acting cylinder turns a locomotive

1. The problem the gear solves

  • The cylinder is double-acting: steam pushes the piston from one end, then from the other, so every stroke does work.
  • Something must admit steam to the correct end at the correct instant, and open the other end to exhaust.
  • That job belongs to the piston valve in the chest above the cylinder, and the valve gear is what drives it.
  • The gear must also let the driver reverse the engine and vary how long steam is admitted — without stopping.

2. Two motions, added together

  • From the wheel: the eccentric crank is set about — from the main crank pin. Through the eccentric rod it rocks the expansion link.
  • From the crosshead: the union link takes motion straight off the piston rod, giving a small component always in step with the piston.
  • The combination lever adds the two and drives the valve spool.
  • The wheel-derived part sets the main timing; the crosshead-derived part supplies lap and lead, so a little steam is admitted just before the piston reaches the end of its stroke.

3. Cut-off and reversing

  • The die block slides along the curved slot in the expansion link. The reversing rod sets where it sits.
  • One side of the slot's centre gives forward gear, the other reverse — the same gear runs the engine either way.
  • Moving the block toward the centre notches up: valve travel shrinks, steam is admitted for a shorter part of the stroke and then expands.
  • Short cut-off means less power but better economy — it is how a driver runs an engine once it is up to speed.
  • At mid gear the block sits on the pivot, the link contributes almost nothing, and only lap and lead remain.

4. Where the steam goes

  • The valve chest is held at boiler pressure; the spools slide within it to uncover the ports.
  • A port opens only once the valve has travelled past its lap, so admission fades in rather than snapping on.
  • Live steam (pink) enters one end of the bore and drives the piston; the other end is opened to exhaust (blue).
  • The valve hands over twice per revolution, which is the pair of chuffs you hear from a single cylinder.
  • Spent steam leaves up the exhaust stack — the plume above the chest.

5. Things to try

  • Drag Reversing across zero: watch the die block cross the link centre and the engine run the other way.
  • Notch toward neutral and watch the valve's travel shrink while the piston keeps its full stroke.
  • Scrub Crank Angle by hand to step through admission, cut-off and exhaust one frame at a time.
