Scotch Yoke — Rotation into Simple Harmonic Motion

A slot, a pin, and why the output is an exact cosine

1. What the mechanism does

  • It turns steady rotation into straight-line reciprocation — back-and-forth travel along one axis.
  • A pin on the crank runs inside a straight slot cut across the yoke.
  • The yoke is held by guides so it can only slide horizontally; the slot lets the pin move freely up and down inside it.
  • So the pin's sideways position is handed to the yoke, while its vertical movement is absorbed by the slot.

2. Why the motion is exactly sinusoidal

  • The pin sits at x = r cos θ from the crank centre, so the yoke's displacement is that cosine.
  • Differentiating: velocity v = −rω sin θ, acceleration a = −rω² cos θ.
  • Which means a = −ω²x: acceleration proportional to displacement and always directed back toward the centre.
  • That relation is the definition of simple harmonic motion. The yoke is a mechanical SHM generator.
  • The stroke is 2r — twice the crank radius — whatever the speed.

3. How it differs from a piston engine

  • A normal slider-crank joins crank to piston with a connecting rod of finite length.
  • That rod swings as it goes, so the piston's motion is only approximately sinusoidal — it hurries one half of the stroke and dawdles the other.
  • The Scotch yoke has no connecting rod at all, so it gives a pure cosine at any crank radius.
  • The trade-off is wear: the pin slides hard against the slot faces instead of turning in a bearing.
  • It is used where honest SHM matters — test rigs, vibration tables, some pumps and valve drives.

4. Reading the graph

  • The curve plots yoke displacement against crank angle across one full turn.
  • The travelling dot marks where the mechanism is right now.
  • Amplitude equals the crank radius, so lengthening the crank stretches the wave vertically without changing its shape.
  • The yoke is fastest where the curve is steepest (mid-stroke, θ = 90° and 270°) and momentarily stationary at the crests, the dead points.

5. Things to try

  • Change the crank radius: the stroke follows as 2r, but the motion stays a cosine.
  • Scrub the crank angle by hand and watch the dot walk along the curve.
  • Watch the yoke pause at each end while the crank keeps turning at a constant rate.
  • Listen: the drive pitch rises at mid-stroke and falls at the dead points, following |sin θ|.
