Grade 8 – 10 (Middle & Upper Secondary Science / Physics / Design & Technology)

Subject: Science / Physics / Design & Technology

Relevant Units / Chapters:

Circular Motion & Rotational Kinematics: Rim Speed from Angular Speed ($v = \omega r$), Why the Speed at a Point Depends on How Far Out It Is, Cutting Speed ($V_c = \pi D n$), Zero Speed at the Axis, Revolutions per Minute and Radians per Second.

A Quantity That Changes While You Work: Cutting Speed as a Consequence Rather Than a Setting, Why Turning a Bar Down Makes the Edge Speed Fall Without Touching the Controls, Reading a Live Value Against a Target, Compensating by Changing Gear Part-Way Through a Job.

Limits of a Machine: Discrete Gearbox Speeds and the Absence of Anything In Between, Choosing the Nearest Available Setting, When the Ideal Speed Lies Outside What the Machine Can Do.

Forces & Materials: Specific Cutting Force ($k_c$) as Force per Unit Area of Chip, Chip Cross-Section as Feed × Depth of Cut, Shearing a Material, Why Steel Demands About Three Times What Aluminium Does.

Turning Effect of a Force (Moments): Torque as Force × Radius ($\tau = F_c \times D \div 2$), Why the Same Cut Loads a Fat Bar More Heavily Than a Thin One, Reading a Demanded Torque Against an Available One.

Work, Power & Machines: Power as Force × Velocity ($P = F_c \times V_c$), Fixed Motor Power as the Binding Constraint, Efficiency of a Drive, Stalling as the Visible Consequence of Asking for More Power Than Exists.

Rates, Speed & Time: Feed per Revolution as Distance per Turn, Time for a Pass as Length ÷ (Feed × Speed), Why Spindle Speed Appears in the Time on a Lathe but Not on a Drill, How the Definition of a Quantity Changes the Relationship It Enters.

Geometry & Proportional Reasoning (Cross-Curricular): Radius versus Diameter, Why a Depth of Cut Taken Off the Radius Halves Twice as Fast in Diameter, Direct Proportion and Straight-Line Graphs Through the Origin, Reading a Point on a Line as a Pair of Values.

Comparing Two Machines: The Same Formula Behaving Differently When One of Its Terms Is Free to Change, Transferring a Method Between Contexts and Checking Whether It Still Holds.

Design & Technology: Anatomy of a Centre Lathe — Bed, Headstock, Chuck, Saddle, Cross-Slide, Toolpost and Tailstock, Which Slide Does Which Job, Work-Holding in a Three-Jaw Chuck, Turning, Facing and Parting Off, Selecting a Speed for a Material and a Diameter, Reading a Machine's Own Speed Chart.

Mathematics (Cross-Curricular): Circumference of a Circle, Ratio and Proportion, Rearranging a Formula to Make a Different Quantity the Subject, Reading Values Against a Working Range, Interpreting a Line Graph and a Point Moving Along It.
