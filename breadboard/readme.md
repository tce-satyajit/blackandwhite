Grade 8 – 10 (Middle & Upper Secondary Science / Physics / Design & Technology)

Subject: Science / Physics / Design & Technology

Relevant Units / Chapters:

Electric Circuits (Foundations): The Circuit as a Closed Loop, Why Breaking It Anywhere Stops It Everywhere, Conventional Current and the Direction It Is Drawn In, The Switch as a Deliberate Gap, Open and Closed Circuits, Circuit Symbols Against the Real Component.

Current, Voltage and Resistance: Current as Rate of Flow of Charge and Why It Is the Same at Every Point of a Series Loop, Potential Difference as an Energy Difference Between Two Points, Resistance as the Ratio of the Two, Ohm's Law ($V = I \times R$) and Rearranging It, Reading a Value Against a Working Range.

Series and Parallel Circuits: Adding Resistances in Series ($R = R_1 + R_2$), Combining Them in Parallel ($1/R = 1/R_1 + 1/R_2$), Why Voltage Divides in Series and Is Shared in Parallel, Why Current Divides in Parallel, Kirchhoff's Voltage Law Around a Loop, Kirchhoff's Current Law at a Junction, Why House Lighting Is Wired in Parallel.

Electrical Energy and Power: Power as the Product of Voltage and Current ($P = V \times I$), Power Dissipated in a Resistor ($P = I^2R$), Where the Energy Goes — Light, Motion, Sound and Heat, Rated Values and What Happens Above Them, Why a Resistor Gets Warm.

Cells, Batteries and Internal Resistance: EMF Against Terminal Potential Difference, Internal Resistance as the Reason the Supply Sags Under Load ($V = \varepsilon - Ir$), Cells in Series and Why Four AA Cells Give Six Volts, Why a Short Circuit Draws a Large but Finite Current.

Components and Their Characteristics: The Non-Ohmic Component, Forward Voltage of a Light-Emitting Diode, Why an LED Cannot Limit Its Own Current, The Current-Limiting Resistor and How Its Value Is Chosen, Polarity and One-Way Conduction, The Variable Resistor as a Continuous Control, Resistor Colour Codes.

Electromagnetic Effects (Motors): The Motor as a Load That Is Not a Resistor, Back-EMF and Why It Rises With Speed, Why a Stalled Motor Draws Far More Current Than a Running One, Why a Motor Needs Current Rather Than Voltage Alone, Torque Against Load.

Measurement and Instruments: The Voltmeter Connected Across a Component, The Ammeter Connected In the Loop, Why the Two Cannot Be Swapped, Choosing a Range, Reading a Digital Display, Systematic Error From an Imperfect Meter.

Practical Electronics & Design and Technology: How a Solderless Breadboard Is Connected Inside, Terminal Strips of Five and the Central Channel, Power Rails and Why the Supply Goes There, Reading a Board's Row Letters and Column Numbers, Building From a Circuit Diagram, Fault-Finding a Circuit That Does Not Work, Component Handling and Polarity Conventions.

Safety and Failure: Recognising a Short Circuit, Why Components Fail Suddenly Rather Than Gradually, Overload as Accumulated Heat Rather Than an Instant Event, Reading a Rated Current Against an Actual One.

Mathematics (Cross-Curricular): Direct and Inverse Proportion, Rearranging a Formula to Make a Different Quantity the Subject, Ratio and Fractions in Current Division, Reciprocals in the Parallel Resistance Rule, Units and Prefixes (milli-, kilo-), Substituting Into a Formula and Checking an Answer Against a Measurement.
