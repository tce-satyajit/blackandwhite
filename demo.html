Walschaerts Valve Gear

How a locomotive times steam into a double-acting cylinder. Adjust cut-off and reversing, watch the piston valve distribute live and exhaust steam, and hear the exhaust beat.

Grade 8–10 · Physics

Rotary to Linear Motion

A crank-rocker driving a toothed rack. Vary the crank length to cross the Grashof limit and see continuous rotation give way to a linkage that can no longer turn.

Grade 8–10 · Physics

Scotch Yoke Mechanism

A pin running in a slotted yoke, turning rotation into exact simple harmonic motion. Watch displacement trace a true cosine while the crank turns steadily.

Grade 8–10 · Physics

Aircraft Landing Gear

Land an airliner and watch the strut absorb it. Momentum, impulse and Hooke’s law, with live force and compression graphs showing why a longer impact means a gentler one.

Grade 9–10 · Physics

Car Gearbox

Start the engine, pick a gear and drive. Watch a small gear turn a big one into strong slow torque — and see on the dials why a hill wants first gear and a motorway wants fifth.

Grade 8–10 · Physics

Differential Gear — 2D

Round a bend the outer wheel has further to go. Watch the spider gears spin to let it, check that the two wheel speeds always average to the carrier — then put one wheel on ice and see why the car goes nowhere.

Grade 8–11 · Physics

Differential Gear — 3D

The same physics on a real three-dimensional differential you can turn round and look into. Hold one wheel still and watch the other run at exactly twice the carrier speed.

Grade 8–11 · Physics

Bench Drill

One motor at one speed, and a belt on five pairs of pulleys to turn it into five. Move the belt and watch speed and turning effort trade off exactly. Then try to stall it: on the low steps the belt slips first, on the high ones the motor gives out — and changing gear turns out to be no help at all against the second one.

Grade 8–10 · Physics

6-Axis Robot Arm

An industrial arm picking parts off a table and setting them on a moving belt. Six revolute joints, solved with the cosine rule; watch the shoulder torque climb as the load goes out to arm's length, and find the acceleration that pulls the part off the tool.

Grade 8–11 · Physics

3D Printer

How a machine builds a solid object by adding one layer at a time. Set the layer height, speed and temperatures, watch the X, Y and Z axes move as the nozzle traces each slice, and print the pot in four colours off the four-spool AMS. Slow motion drops it to real time, to watch a single line actually go down.
