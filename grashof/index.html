Grashof's Law — Converting Rotation into Linear Motion

Why this linkage turns a spinning crank into a straight-line stroke

1. What the law says

  • A four-bar chain has four link lengths. Call the shortest s, the longest l, and the remaining two p and q.
  • If s + l ≤ p + q, the chain is a Grashof linkage: at least one link can turn a full 360°.
  • If s + l > p + q, it is non-Grashof: no link completes a revolution, every moving link merely rocks.
  • The law decides only whether continuous rotation is possible — not how fast or how far anything moves.

2. Which mechanism you get

  • Crank-rocker — the shortest link is one of the two joined to the fixed link. That link rotates fully; the opposite one oscillates. This page.
  • Double-crank (drag-link) — the shortest link is the fixed one. Both links joined to it rotate fully.
  • Double-rocker — the shortest link is the coupler. Neither grounded link rotates fully, though the coupler does.
  • Triple-rocker — the non-Grashof case. Everything oscillates, nothing revolves.

3. This linkage

  • Fixed link (support) —, connecting rod —, rocker arm —, crank adjustable from the slider.
  • Grashof holds while the crank stays at or below —. Beyond that, s + l overtakes p + q.
  • Within that range the crank is the shortest link and is joined to the fixed link → crank-rocker.
  • So the crank turns continuously while the rocker only swings back and forth through a fixed arc.

4. Turning the swing into straight-line travel

  • The rocker carries a toothed sector of pitch radius — that meshes with a straight rack.
  • Contact is pure rolling, so the rack travels the arc length the sector sweeps: stroke = R × swing (swing in radians).
  • A guide sleeve holds the rack to one axis, so the output is a clean reciprocating line.
  • Net result: continuous rotation in → reciprocating straight-line motion out.
  • Only part of the rim needs teeth — the sector never makes a full turn, so the rest is plain.

5. Things to try

  • Lengthen the crank: the rocker's swing widens and the rack's stroke grows with it.
  • Push past the Grashof limit — the crank jams part-way round, the readout flips to Non-Grashof and a warning appears.
  • Switch on Motion Paths to see the crank's complete circle against the rocker's partial arc, with the stroke marked on the rack.
  • Scrub the crank angle by hand to watch the rack reverse at each end of the swing.
