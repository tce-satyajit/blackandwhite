Differential Gear — How Both Wheels Turn at Different Speeds

Learning outcomes, challenges and the physics behind the model

1. The problem a differential solves

  • Going round a bend, the outer wheel travels along a bigger circle than the inner one.
  • Arc length is s = Rθ, so in the same time the outer wheel must cover more ground — it has to turn faster.
  • Lock both wheels to one shaft and one of them must skid. The differential lets them differ while still driving both.

2. Learning outcomes

  • Explain why the two driven wheels of a cornering car cannot turn at the same speed.
  • Use s = Rθ and v = ωr to link path radius, road speed and wheel speed.
  • State and test the differential rule: the average of the two wheel speeds is always the carrier speed.
  • Identify the ring gear, carrier, spider gears and side gears, and say what each one does.
  • Explain that an open differential splits torque equally, whatever the two wheels are doing.
  • Predict what happens when one wheel is on ice, and explain why the car goes nowhere.
  • Distinguish a speed rule (the average) from a torque rule (the equal split) — the two are not the same thing.
  • Recognise that a machine can share motion out unevenly without creating any extra energy.

3. Try this

Tick marks appear as you manage each one. Reset clears them.

  • ☐ 1. Drive dead straight. Are the two wheel speeds equal? Watch the spider gears — do they spin at all?
  • ☐ 2. Take a tight turn. Make the outer wheel turn at least 1.4× as fast as the inner one.
  • ☐ 3. Check the rule. Hold a turn for a few seconds and watch (left + right) ÷ 2 stay equal to the carrier speed.
  • ☐ 4. Turn as tightly as you can. Show that a tighter bend makes a bigger speed difference.
  • ☐ 5. Press Left Wheel on Ice and try to drive away. Why does the car stay put while a wheel spins?

4. What the parts do

  • Ring gear — driven by the propeller shaft. Everything the engine sends arrives here.
  • Carrier — bolted to the ring gear, so it turns with it. It carries the spider gears round.
  • Spider gears — ride on the carrier and mesh with both side gears. Going straight they do not spin at all: they simply carry. In a turn they spin, and that spin is exactly what lets one wheel run faster than the other.
  • Side gears — one for each half-shaft, so one for each wheel.
Physics behind the simulation

s = Rθ

Arc length. Sweeping the same angle on a bigger radius covers more distance.

vinner = v (R − t/2) / R    vouter = v (R + t/2) / R

Both wheels sweep the same angle per second, so their speeds are in the same ratio as their radii. t is the track width.

v = ω r

Road speed of a rolling wheel, from its turning rate and its radius.

ωleft + ωright = 2 ωcarrier

The differential rule. The carrier speed is always the average of the two wheel speeds — in a corner, on ice, or standing still. Whatever one wheel gains, the other loses.

τleft = τright = τring / 2

An open differential shares torque equally, always. This is a different rule from the speed one, and it is the reason the ice trick works.

τgrip = μ N r

The most torque a tyre can pass to the road before it slips. On ice μ is tiny, so τgrip is tiny — and because the split is equal, the good wheel is held down to that same tiny value.

F = τ / r    a = F / m

Wheel torque becomes a push at the road, and the net push accelerates the car.

Assumptions, and what is left out

  • The mechanism drawing is a schematic. Real side and spider gears are bevel gears meeting at right angles; they are drawn flat here so every part can be seen turning at once.
  • The differential is open — no limited-slip or locking device. That is why one icy wheel stops the car.
  • Weight is shared equally by the two wheels. A real car shifts load onto the outer wheel in a corner.
  • Tyres roll without sideways slip, and the car follows the radius the steering asks for.
  • Speed is capped by cornering grip, v ≤ √(μgR) — a real car would slide out of the bend above that.
  • Gear teeth are drawn evenly and do not mesh tooth-for-tooth; the speeds they turn at are the accurate part.
